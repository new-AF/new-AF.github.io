Stop Gatekeeping Referrals

In the tech industry

Abdullah Fatota
Updated: 28 April 2026

Overview

Referrals are one of the quickest and surest ways to get an interview at a company. Why we need referrals? because recruiters are terrible at their job and keep wasting time chasing the illusive "perfect" candidate, so we have to find a quicker and more efficient way.

And because the process of breaking into Tech is hard, those who did make it tend to exhibit some unormal behavior to say the least. For example I recently saw a blog post from a software engineer and their takeaway was:

Don't DM on LinkedIn with a generic "Do you have open positions at your company" instead show a genuine interest in me/my expertise first.

I mean what they're supposed to do? fake interest in your career and the intricacies of your position.

If you want people to go beyond, to "standout", to put in time and effort stalk your profile, blogs and career and use that information in a personalized message then I think you are soliciting the wrong type of attention.

Sure maybe that 0.01% of those sending you request connections will be genuinely interested in your journey but the remaining 99.99% simply want to get a job, and it doesn't mean they won't excel at it.

And the worst thing is that engineer like everyone in Tech knows that, but they have a problem with the theatrics of it all how the message is worded, rather than genuine intent.

Story time

Sometime ago as well, I DMed a fellow engineer for referral and their reply was:

Sorry I don't know you personally or professionally, but wish you best of luck.

Mind you we attended the same university classes, even after I pointed that out and invited them to ask anything they like, they declined. Of course I'm not entitled me to someone's labor, its theirs to give or deny, that goes without saying.

But beyond these isolated incidents, Tech has developed a toxic culture of gate-keeping and lack of empathy that is making real life harm that manifests in two ugly ways:

  1. Slide to fascism this includes the ongoing Gaza genocide, ICE terror, rise of white-supremacy, racism, xenophobia, all the bad things drastically reduce the quality of life for everyone.

  2. Layoffs, ironically the same gate-keeping culture that is supposed to protect you, makes it that much easier to get rid of you.

The ongoing Gaza genocide

The ongoing holocaust in Gaza powered by Amazon, Microsoft and Google Cloud can only keep going because of the lack of empathy from its engineers as it seemingly does not affect them, unbeknownst to them fueling this cruelty culture will be becoming for them too.

Layoffs

Predictably however, this culture has come for everyone in the form of now normalized layoffs, for example in 2025 alone:

  • genocidal Microsoft laid off 9,100 workers in July most in Tech despite being profitable.

  • genocidal Amazon laid off 14,000 workers in October despite being super profitable.

Let's be clear the main reason these layoffs are happening is because these companies can, or rather they are acutely aware of Tech's lack of empathy and solidarity culture and learned they will face zero consequences if they mass fire their employees.

Because if it's not the norm to mass protest your company's enabling of an ongoing genocide, realistically your colleagues will not stop their work and join an organized sit-in when you lose your job.

My referral process

I like things simple and systemic, so here's my process:

  1. It's Free Palestine or we can't be friends. If you think someone who is pro-genocide will be bring boon to your business you will learn the hard way.

  2. Show me anything you coded, this could be your GitHub, CodePen, SourceForge, a project on your laptop, a Website (I'm biased in this regard 😅), etc.

  3. You're 98% there, you look like a decent human being and skilled engineer. Finally, I will look at your social media (I don't tolerate bigotry, racism, xenophobia or calls to harm people)

Congratulations! I will pass your CV/name in front of the key people I know. Needless to say I can't guarantee you will be hired but I can guarantee I have referred you.

(also needless to say you can always game these criteria in which case I reserve the right to retroactively take back the endorsement)

Conclusion

If you want things to improve in Tech and the world you live in, you got to stop gatekeeping your referrals, if you think it protects you because someone might take your job, it simply isolates you and makes you susceptible to a layoff or reduced quality of life, which will get to you long before a new hire comes close.
